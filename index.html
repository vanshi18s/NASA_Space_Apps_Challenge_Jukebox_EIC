Earth Jukebox

NASA’s Earth data was made to be seen. Choose a dataset and hear how our planet is changing.

How the sound is made

Latitude → pitch
12 bands from 70°N to 50°S in an A minor pentatonic scale. North is high, south is low.
Light → loudness
Denser, brighter lights are louder, on one fixed scale for every year. Gray land is ignored. On the map, the glow beside the gold bar shows the loudness of each latitude band.
Bright hubs
The brightest clusters ring like a bell when the scan reaches them and stay marked on the map. A brighter hub rings louder, and its pitch follows latitude.
Longitude → time and stereo
The scan moves west to east and the sound moves from the left ear to the right.
Compare 2012 with another year
Pick any year from 2013 to 2023. 2012 is shown above that year, and 2012 is scanned first, then the chosen year. One soft blip marks 2012 and two blips mark the chosen year. 2012 can never be compared with itself.
Year tone
Each year’s total light sets how bright the sound is. Years are scaled between the dimmest and brightest of 2012–2023, so 2012 sounds dull and soft and 2023 bright and full.
Intensity
Master volume only: Soft (−10 dB), Medium (−4 dB) or Full (0 dB). It never changes the data mapping, so the sound is the same shape, just quieter or louder.
Stop and resume
Stop pauses the scan. Play then says Resume and continues from the same spot. Changing mode, dataset or year, or pressing Home, starts over.
Year label
The year printed in NASA’s video is never analysed. It sits outside the map area we read, and that spot is also masked.

Disclaimer

Earth Jukebox is an independent project made for the NASA International Space Apps Challenge. NASA does not endorse this project or any non-U.S. Government entity and is not responsible for the content of non-U.S. Government websites.

Imagery comes from NASA’s Scientific Visualization Studio (SVS), including “Change in Night Lights between 2012 and 2023 – EIC Version” (SVS 5313), based on NASA Black Marble data from Suomi NPP VIIRS. We turn NASA’s published visualizations into sound. The frames are display-stretched pictures, not calibrated measurements, so the sound shows patterns and direction of change, not exact values. Choices such as the musical scale and instrument tones are artistic; what is loud, high or rising comes from the data. Do not use it for scientific analysis or safety decisions.

Sea-surface data: “Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature” (SVS 3912), NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. It shows model output from ECCO2 (MIT/JPL) from 25 March 2007 to 3 March 2008: colours are sea surface temperature and flow lines are surface currents. Animators: Greg Shirah, Horace Mitchell and Eric Sokolowsky. Producer: Michael Starobin. Scientists: Hong Zhang (UCLA) and Dimitris Menemenlis (NASA JPL/Caltech). Temperatures here are read back from the colour bar of the picture, so they are approximate and not model values.

All sound is synthesized live in your browser with the Web Audio API; no recordings are used. Font: Atkinson Hyperlegible (Braille Institute) and Unbounded, under the SIL Open Font License. This code was written with assistance from Claude (Anthropic).
